## Details

### D1. Burning solids release CO - detect gases, not only smoke

- **Finding:** In 118 BASS-II tests (small solid samples in the ISS glovebox) CO rose by a median 45 ppm, 90th percentile 413 ppm, maximum 575 ppm per test.
- **Action:** Pair smoke detection with CO (and CO2) sensing in crew volumes; toxic gas, not heat, killed the Apollo 1 crew.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 118; confidence medium
- **Effect:** median CO rise per test = 45
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-25 BASS-II Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov)
- **Linked:** H1, H3

### S3. Less oxygen makes flames go out earlier

- **Finding:** Lowering O2 from 21 % to 15 % changes the droplet size at flame extinction (relative to the initial size) by +50 % (95 % CI +34 % to +79 %; 188 FLEX tests with extinction): more fuel is left unburned.
- **Action:** Reducing O2 (venting, inert flooding) is an effective way to shorten a microgravity fire.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 188; confidence high
- **Effect:** d_ext/d0 ratio, O2 0.21 -> 0.15 = 1.5 [1.34, 1.79]
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-69 FLEX Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov), NASA/TP-2015-216046
- **Linked:** H3

### P1. More oxygen burns fuel faster

- **Finding:** Raising O2 from 21 % to 30 % (other conditions fixed) changes the droplet burning rate by +44 % (95 % CI +39 % to +55 %), from 257 FLEX tests on the ISS.
- **Action:** Treat every percent of extra O2 in a habitat as a fire-hazard increase; tighten material screening and ignition-source control when O2 is raised above sea-level air.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 257; confidence high
- **Effect:** K ratio, O2 0.21 -> 0.30 = 1.44 [1.39, 1.55]
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-69 FLEX Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov), NASA/TP-2015-216046; Regression with regime bootstrap: src/fif/insights/catalog.py
- **Linked:** H1

### S4. At 30 % O2 droplet flames put themselves out less often

- **Finding:** N2-diluted FLEX tests: the flame burned the droplet out without extinguishing in 54 % of 13 tests at O2 >= 30 %, vs 14 % of 58 tests at O2 <= 21 %.
- **Action:** At elevated O2 assume a fire will not self-extinguish; active suppression must be immediate.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 71; confidence medium
- **Effect:** share without extinction, O2>=0.30 / O2<=0.21 = 3.9
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-69 FLEX Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov), NASA/TP-2015-216046
- **Linked:** H1

### P4. In O2-rich streams gaseous fuels ignite almost every time

- **Finding:** ACME CFI-G inverse flames on the ISS: ignition in 98 % of 101 attempts with oxidizer O2 >= 50 % (up to 85 %), vs 67 % of 33 attempts below 30 %.
- **Action:** Treat O2 lines, regulators and O2-generator outlets as top-priority ignition hazards (keep hydrocarbons and electrical heat sources away from them).
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 134; confidence medium
- **Effect:** ignition share O2>=0.5 vs <0.3 = 1.47
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-159 ACME CFI-G Experimental Tables (https://psi.nasa.gov)
- **Linked:** H2, H3

### S2. Helium dilution does not slow burning - it speeds it up

- **Finding:** Replacing 30 % of the nitrogen by helium changes the burning rate by +35 % (95 % CI +30 % to +42 %; 257 FLEX tests): helium conducts heat to the droplet faster.
- **Action:** Helium is not a substitute for CO2 as a fire-suppressing diluent; account for it if He is used as a cabin diluent.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 257; confidence high
- **Effect:** K ratio, 30 % N2 -> He = 1.35 [1.3, 1.42]
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-69 FLEX Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov), NASA/TP-2015-216046

### P2. A Moon-base atmosphere burns droplets faster than ISS air

- **Finding:** Hybrid model, 3 mm droplet: Moon-base atmosphere (O2 30 %, 10.2 psi) vs ISS cabin air (O2 21 %, 1 atm): methanol +32 % (95 % band -2 % to +77 %); heptane +32 % (95 % band -2 % to +79 %). Label: extrapolation (O2 30 % was tested only from 0.703 atm).
- **Action:** Size fire-detection response and suppression capacity of Moon-base modules and rovers for faster fuel consumption than on the ISS; re-test cabin materials at the planned atmosphere.
- **Evidence:** model; n = 274; confidence low; extrapolation
- **Effect:** K ratio Moon/ISS (heptane) = 1.32 [0.983, 1.79]
- **Sources:** Challenge page 'Flame in Freefall' (docs/challenge/CHALLENGE.md) (Moon-base atmosphere); NASA/TP-2015-216046 Table I (ISS cabin air); models/K_hybrid.joblib
- **Linked:** H1, H2

### S1. CO2 dilution slows burning

- **Finding:** Replacing 30 % of the nitrogen by CO2 (same O2 and pressure) changes the burning rate by -7 % (95 % CI -11 % to -3 %; 257 FLEX tests).
- **Action:** CO2 is an effective diluent suppressant in microgravity; keep it in the suppression design.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 257; confidence high
- **Effect:** K ratio, 30 % N2 -> CO2 = 0.927 [0.885, 0.972]
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-69 FLEX Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov), NASA/TP-2015-216046

### P3. Lowering cabin pressure barely slows burning

- **Finding:** Going from 1.0 to 0.7 atm at the same O2 fraction changes the burning rate by -3 % (95 % CI -7 % to +0 %; 257 FLEX tests).
- **Action:** Do not count on reduced habitat pressure to offset the added hazard of a higher O2 fraction.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 257; confidence low
- **Effect:** K ratio, 1.0 -> 0.7 atm = 0.971 [0.928, 1]
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-69 FLEX Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov), NASA/TP-2015-216046
- **Linked:** P2

### D3. Smoke from spacecraft materials has been measured in microgravity

- **Finding:** SAME-R ran 109 smoke tests on the ISS with 5 materials (Kapton, Lampwick, Pyrell, Silicone, Teflon) - the data base for smoke detectors tuned to microgravity smoke.
- **Action:** Use SAME/SAME-R particle data when specifying detectors for new spacecraft rather than terrestrial detector standards.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 109; confidence medium
- **Effect:** materials tested = 5
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-101 SAME-R Experimental Table (https://psi.nasa.gov)
- **Linked:** E2

### D2. Cool flames: fuel can keep reacting after the visible flame is gone

- **Finding:** Cool flames were recorded in 8 of 179 ACME CFI-G normal-flame tests; FLEX test notes report cool-flame burning after visible-flame extinction of heptane droplets (NASA/TP-2015-216046).
- **Action:** Do not declare a fire out on optical (visible/UV flame) evidence alone; confirm with gas sensing and temperature.
- **Evidence:** NASA data; n = 8; confidence medium
- **Effect:** cool-flame observations = 8
- **Sources:** NASA PSI-159 ACME CFI-G (https://psi.nasa.gov); NASA/TP-2015-216046 test notes
- **Linked:** H3

### P5. On the lunar surface a droplet burns between its ISS and Earth rates

- **Finding:** Moon-base air (O2 0.30, 10.2 psi), 3 mm droplet at 1/6 g: the burning rate lies between the microgravity rate and 1.46x (methanol) / 1.44x (heptane) that rate (Law & Williams buoyancy correction, Gr = 0.76 / 0.69, inside its fitted range Gr <= 1). Lower bound = microgravity; no partial-gravity data in PSI.
- **Action:** Size Moon-base fire response for a burning rate up to the upper bound, and treat ISS-derived burn times as the slow limit.
- **Evidence:** physics theory; n = 0; confidence low; extrapolation
- **Effect:** K(1/6 g)/K(0 g) upper bound (heptane) = 1.44
- **Sources:** Law & Williams, Combust. Flame 19 (1972) Eq. (9); docs/PHYSICS.md section 4; NTRS 20250004015 (lunar gravity = 1/6 of Earth gravity)
- **Linked:** P2, D4, P6

### P6. NASA: for solid materials, lunar gravity may be the most flammable condition

- **Finding:** NASA Glenn reports that "Lunar gravity is nearly the most flammable condition" for solid materials (flame spread peaks and the oxygen limit is lowest near lunar g), and a partial-g habitat fire is expected to be "substantially worse than in 0-g and potentially worse than even 1-g". The FM2 experiment is to measure this on the Moon.
- **Action:** Do not assume a material that passes the 1-g screening test is safe in a Moon base; add margin until lunar data (FM2) exist.
- **Evidence:** NASA literature; n = 0; confidence medium
- **Effect:** - = n/a
- **Sources:** NTRS 20210020516 (Fire on the Moon, NASA GRC); NTRS 20260000646 (Ferkul et al., LPSC 2026)
- **Linked:** P5, H1

### D4. Without buoyancy the same droplet burns slower than on Earth

- **Finding:** Law & Williams buoyancy correction for a 3 mm droplet in air at 1 atm: Earth burning rate is 3.0x (methanol) and 2.9x (heptane) the microgravity rate (correlation used far outside its fitted range, Gr = 13).
- **Action:** Do not transfer Earth flammability or detection-time results to spacecraft without microgravity data; microgravity flames are smaller and slower, so they may be detected later.
- **Evidence:** physics theory; n = 0; confidence low; extrapolation
- **Effect:** K_Earth/K_ISS (heptane) = 2.92
- **Sources:** Law & Williams, Combust. Flame 19 (1972) Eq. (9); docs/PHYSICS.md section 4

## Lessons from history (NASA history, R2-R4)

- **Apollo 1: pure oxygen turned a spark into a flash fire.** "Because of the pure oxygen atmosphere, the fire spread quickly"; the crew died of "asphyxia from carbon monoxide and other toxic gases" (NASA SMA). Our data show the same physics at milder O2 levels (P1, S4) and the CO hazard (D1). Source: https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/apollo-1
- **Apollo 13: oxygen system plus electrical heat.** Heaters in the oxygen tank overheated, "degrading the Teflon insulation"; the redesign sheathed wiring "in stainless steel" (NASA SMA). O2-rich hardware is where ignition-source control matters most (P4). Source: https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/apollo-13
- **Mir 1997: a real fire in microgravity.** An oxygen generator burned; "The flame blocked the evacuation route to the Soyuz spacecraft"; smoke was cleared "over several hours" (NASA History). Microgravity fires produce lasting smoke and gases (D1, D2) and can cut off escape routes: detection and immediate suppression (S3) are critical. Source: https://www.nasa.gov/history/25-years-ago-fire-aboard-space-station-mir/

## Benefits on Earth (NASA spinoff, R5-R7)

- **Lighter firefighter air tanks.** Developed with NASA Johnson Space Center: "20 pounds for 30 minute air supply" (NASA Spinoff, 1976). Benefit on Earth of NASA life-support work. Source: https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9582
- **Incipient fire detector from the Space Shuttle.** Signals fire "before appearance of flame and smoke" and is "less susceptible to false alarms" (NASA Spinoff, 1978). Space detection technology already protects mines and buildings; SAME data (D3) can do the same. Source: https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9407
- **Fire-resistant gear from Apollo materials.** Project FIRES used "lightweight, fire-resistant, heat-protective materials" from space suits (NASA Spinoff, 1982). Materials screened for O2-rich cabins protect firefighters on Earth. Source: https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9918
